Search for new mid-level through senior AI/ML engineering roles across Portugal, Spain, France, the United Kingdom, and selected high-value European remote opportunities.

The goal is not simply to collect job listings.

The goal is to identify high-quality AI engineering opportunities that are realistically actionable from Lisbon, understand the companies and teams behind them, identify the most strategically relevant people associated with those opportunities, and generate genuinely personalized outreach based on real public signals such as technical work, talks, research, open-source contributions, events, articles, product launches, engineering challenges, or shared professional interests.

Prioritize quality over quantity.

10. Networking signal quality

For every person considered for outreach, classify the personalization signal:

STRONG

There is a specific relevant piece of work, article, talk, project, research, event, or technical topic that directly overlaps with the open role or candidate's interests.

GOOD

There is meaningful professional overlap, but not a highly specific artifact.

WEAK

Only generic information such as job title or company employment is available.

Prefer STRONG and GOOD contacts.

Do not create a heavily personalized message from a WEAK signal.

If there is no meaningful personalization signal, say:

No strong outreach angle found — better to apply directly.

This is preferable to generating generic networking spam.

12. Personalized outreach philosophy

Messages should create a natural professional reason for communication.

The primary goal is NOT:

"Can we schedule 15 minutes?"

The primary goal is:

"I saw something specific you worked on, it genuinely connects to something I'm interested in/building/exploring, and I also noticed your team is hiring for a relevant position."

The best message should make replying feel natural without requiring the recipient to commit to a meeting.

13. Outreach message structure

Whenever a STRONG or GOOD personalization signal exists, create a concise message following approximately this structure:

1. Specific hook

Mention the person's actual work, article, talk, research, open-source project, event, technical comment, or initiative.

2. Genuine connection

Briefly explain why that caught the candidate's attention.

Prefer technical specificity.

3. Vacancy connection

Naturally mention the relevant open role.

4. Easy conversational opening

End with a lightweight question, observation, or point of curiosity that can be answered asynchronously.

Do not immediately request:

Coffee

A meeting

A referral

15 minutes

A call

An introduction

The conversation can evolve toward those outcomes later if the person responds.

14. Good networking-message example

Prefer messages conceptually like:

Hey Sarah — I came across your talk on evaluating agent workflows at [event]. I liked the point you made about treating tool-call success separately from final-answer quality; I've been exploring a similar evaluation setup for production agents. I also noticed the Applied AI Engineer opening on your team, which looks very aligned with the systems I enjoy working on. Curious whether evaluation is still one of the harder problems the team is investing in this year.

Instead of:

Hi Sarah! I saw you're an engineer at Company X and noticed you're hiring. I'd love to learn more about the team. Would you have 15 minutes for a coffee/call?

Avoid messages that could have been sent unchanged to 100 other employees.
